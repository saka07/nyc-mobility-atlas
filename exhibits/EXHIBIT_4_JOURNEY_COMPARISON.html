Rosedale to Lower Manhattan (M02)
61.9 min scheduled total		 Line-haul commuter rail is fast (LIRR covers 14 miles in 27.0 min). The primary bottleneck is an 8.5-minute outdoor street walk between Nostrand Ave stations combined with paying two separate fares. Target: Weather-sheltered transfer connection and unified OMNY fare integration. MONITORED SEPARATELY: LIRR operates under commuter rail on-time standards (within 5.99 min). Q111 feeder bus has an 8.54 mph AM speed and 69.89% Wait Assessment.
Throggs Neck to Grand Central (M07)
71.2 min scheduled total		 The feeder bus leg takes 16.0 minutes in mixed traffic along Crosby Avenue, followed by a 42.5-minute express subway ride. Bus priority along Crosby Ave directly addresses feeder delay. (Note: Planned Metro-North Penn Station Access terminates on the West Side, not Grand Central). UNMEASURED: Route Bx8 speed and reliability are missing from the 29-route audit feed. A scheduled 16-minute bus trip does not prove traffic delay.
Key Government Takeaways:
1. Intermodal Friction: Mainline subway and commuter rail legs are fast; travel resistance is concentrated in first-mile walking access (Fresh Meadows) and physical/fare transfer penalties (Rosedale).
2. Corridor Specificity: Bus priority is effective where feeder bus runs are long (Throggs Neck), but has negligible leverage where the bus ride is already short (Fresh Meadows).
