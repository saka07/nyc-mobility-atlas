Exhibit 6: Strategic Decision Matrix & Capital Governance

Capital investments must satisfy statutory restrictions and pass strict empirical screening

1. Statutory Revenue Dedication (PAL § 553-j)

Net congestion pricing revenues are strictly pledged to MTA Capital Program financing:

  • 80% NYC Transit: Subway, bus rapid transit, and station capital assets.
  • 10% Long Island Rail Road: Commuter rail stations, rolling stock, and track capacity.
  • 10% Metro-North Railroad: Regional rail improvements including Penn Station Access.

Statutory Prohibition: Recurring operating expenses (e.g., driver wages) are legally prohibited.

2. Economic Reality: Induced Demand & Sustainable Relief

The Fundamental Law of Highway Congestion (Duranton & Turner 2011):
Expanding highway capacity into an urban core induces latent driving demand (elasticity ≈ 1.0), returning roadways to congested equilibrium.

Role of Pricing and Transit:
Congestion pricing directly manages core demand. However, to sustain modal shift, public transit must offer competitive door-to-door alternatives from outer-borough origins.

4. Three Mandatory Capital Screening Pre-Conditions

  1. Empirical Telemetry Baseline: Collect automated AVL/APC speed and Wait Assessment data on candidate feeder routes before committing capital.
  2. CBD Destination Verification: Confirm what proportion of corridor drivers travel specifically into the tolled CBD (south of 60th St) versus Upper Manhattan.
  3. First-Mile Pedestrian Audit: Verify whether proposed capital improvements address the dominant journey barrier (e.g. walking access vs in-vehicle bus delay).
Key Government Takeaways:
1. Statutory Governance: Congestion pricing revenue cannot fund everyday bus operating subsidies; interventions must be permanent capital infrastructure programmed into MTA budgets.
2. Empirical Gatekeeping: Investments must target demonstrated corridor bottlenecks rather than assumed delays, ensuring public funds achieve verifiable commuter benefits.
Statutory Framework: New York Public Authorities Law Section 553-j.
Economic Literature: Duranton & Turner (2011), The Fundamental Law of Road Congestion, American Economic Review.
