Exhibit 6 · What to do

Each area needs a different fix: start in Eastern Queens, then the East Bronx and Southeast Queens

One main problem and one main fix in each place, the order of work, and the three decisions we ask for.

1. The fix in each place

Place Main problem What to build Phase
Fresh Meadows
Eastern Queens		Long walks to the bus (the bus itself is short and on time)Safer, shorter walks to the bus stops, and better shelters1
Union Turnpike
Eastern Queens		A long bus ride (14 to 41 minutes) and an 8-minute change to the subwayBus lanes and bus priority at traffic signals, and a sheltered transfer at Kew Gardens1
Throggs Neck
East Bronx		A slow, often late bus (7.9 mph; 67% on time)A bus lane and bus priority on Crosby Avenue, after a 2026 speed check2
Rosedale
Southeast Queens		An outdoor walk between trains and a second fareA covered walkway at Nostrand Avenue, and a cheaper transfer fare2

2. Why Eastern Queens first

Most zone workers. 2,649 residents of the Eastern Queens area work in the zone, against 1,454 in the East Bronx area and 651 in the Southeast Queens area.

Biggest time saved. A faster Union Turnpike bus saves 8.7 minutes a trip on average. The same test saves about 3.7 minutes in Throggs Neck, because most of that trip is on the 6 train.

The East Bronx comes next. It has more people driving alone (57%) and a slow, often late bus. Current bus speeds should confirm the delay before building.

3. Three decisions we ask for

  1. Approve Phase 1 in Eastern Queens: the Union Turnpike bus, safer walks in Fresh Meadows, and a better transfer at Kew Gardens.
  2. Add both local buses to speed tracking: the Fresh Meadows and Throggs Neck buses, every month.
  3. Agree how success will be judged, before work starts (see box 4).

4. How we will know it worked

  1. Trip time from door to door is shorter.
  2. Riders on the improved buses go up.
  3. Car entries into the zone from the area go down.
  4. Emissions go down.
  5. Each change is compared with Hillside Avenue, a nearby road left unchanged, so other causes are ruled out.

What this shows

  • Each place has its own main problem, so one fix does not suit all three.
  • Eastern Queens has the most zone workers and the largest time saved, so it goes first.

What this does not show

  • How many drivers would switch. The pilot would measure this.
  • The places listed are proposals to test, not approved projects.

Sources: U.S. Census employment data (2023) and American Community Survey; trip-planner model; MTA bus speeds and wait assessment; what-if calculation. Phases and decisions are proposals by the study team.
