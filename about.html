Congestion pricing cut traffic. Where should transit money go next?

Traffic into the zone is down. This site shows who still drives in from the outer boroughs, why transit is slow for them, and where a first fix would help most.

How I got these numbers

Four steps, from raw public data to a recommendation.

  1. Measure the result.Compare vehicle entries before and after tolling, and highway speeds in the same months a year apart.
  2. Find who travels.Match where zone workers live (census tracts) with how many drive (survey and census data).
  3. Trace the trip.Model door-to-door journeys and split each into walking, waiting and riding to see what is slow.
  4. Test a fix.Re-run Eastern Queens trips with faster buses and easier transfers, across 72 combinations of origin, destination and departure time.

What this does not show

  • Jobs show where workers live, not whether they drive.
  • Car trips are counted to all of Manhattan, not only the tolled zone.
  • The Union Turnpike savings come from a model. They are not observed results.
  • Speeds on the Q30 and Bx8 feeder buses have not been measured, so those fixes need a data check first.
  • The 12 crossing counts add to 511,829 a weekday and use a different method from the official totals.
