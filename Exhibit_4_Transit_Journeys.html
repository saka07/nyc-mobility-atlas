Exhibit 4 · Door-to-door transit trips

Transit trips take about an hour; walking and waiting take 13–22 minutes of it

Modelled trips on the fall 2026 schedule, leaving 8 a.m. on a Wednesday. These are scheduled times, not observed commutes.

Where the time goes (minutes)

Fresh Meadows → Midtown East

55.6 min

Q30 bus + F express

17.7 min is walking (32% of the trip). The Q30 ride is only 6.9 min, so bus lanes alone save little. Better: shorter, safer walks and a closer route.

Data: Q30 speed and reliability are not in the 29-route data yet.

Rosedale → Lower Manhattan

61.9 min

LIRR + A train

The LIRR covers 14 miles in 27 min, then an 8.5-min outdoor walk between the Nostrand Ave stations and a second fare. Better: a sheltered link and one fare.

Data: LIRR is measured by commuter-rail standards. The Q111 feeder averages 8.5 mph and is on time about 70% of the time.

Throggs Neck → Grand Central

71.2 min

Bx8 bus + 6 express

16 min on the Bx8 along Crosby Ave, then 42.5 min on the 6 express. Better: bus priority on Crosby Ave, once delays are measured.

Data: Bx8 is not in the data. A 16-min scheduled ride does not prove traffic delay.

What this shows

  • Train and subway legs are fast; most lost time is walking to stops, waiting and changing.
  • Walking and waiting: 20.2 min (Fresh Meadows), 22.4 min (Rosedale), 12.7 min (Throggs Neck).
  • Bus lanes help most where the bus ride is long (Throggs Neck), and little where it is short (Fresh Meadows).

Read with care

  • These are model results from published schedules, not measured trips.
  • Driving times are not shown because they were not calibrated.
  • Metro-North’s planned Penn Station Access serves the West Side, not Grand Central.

Source: OpenTripPlanner v2 with MTA fall 2026 schedules (OTP_VERIFIED_10_OD_ITINERARIES.csv, trips M01, M02 and M07).
